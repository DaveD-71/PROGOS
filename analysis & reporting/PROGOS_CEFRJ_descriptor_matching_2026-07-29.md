# PROGOS Report Comments Compared With CEFR-J Descriptors

Prepared: 2026-07-29

## Purpose

This file repeats the earlier CEFR descriptor-alignment check against the CEFR-J descriptor set now available in `cefrj/descriptors/`.

The reason for this second check is methodological: PROGOS public materials refer to CEFR alignment, while related public material also connects PROGOS or the relevant English-level framework to CEFR-J. Because the public materials do not clearly state when report comments are based on CEFR descriptors versus CEFR-J descriptors, both descriptor sets should be checked before drawing conclusions about descriptor alignment. Neither CEFR nor CEFR-J should be prioritised by default.

- PROGOS 2026-07-06 report-comment file: `test reports/Aozawa/0706/report/PROGOS Aozawa #2_0706_evaluation_comments_bilingual.md`

## Public-Source Basis For Checking Both

The public sources do not support treating either CEFR or CEFR-J as the only comparator.

- The PROGOS trial page reviewed on 2026-07-29 describes the test as CEFR-aligned, but the feedback-sheet section also labels overall evaluation as `CEFR準拠(CEFR-J)` and says the assessment result corresponds to `CEFR/CEFR-J`.
- The same trial page describes the indicator-level evaluation as six CEFR-defined speaking-quality items and refers to Can-Do definitions at the relevant CEFR level.
- A 2021 PROGOS release describes PROGOS as CEFR-compliant and based on CEFR-J, and another 2021 PROGOS release says PROGOS complies with CEFR while quantifying speaking ability in levels based on CEFR-J.
- The RareJob 2020 outline PDF separates the level framework in a way that is useful but still not fully sufficient for report-comment matching: it lists `Overall: CEFR & CEFR-J Levels`, `Analytical: 6 aspects of spoken language`, and later shows `CEFR & CEFR-J` for overall levels alongside `CEFR Levels` for the six analytic categories.

Therefore, the safest method is not to choose one framework in advance. The report should check both sets, then state which PROGOS comment aligns with which descriptor set, if either.

## Method

The English translations of the PROGOS report comments were compared with the CEFR-J English speaking descriptors. The CEFR-J English descriptor PDF contains speaking descriptors for `Spoken interaction` and `Spoken production`, subdivided into levels from `PreA1` through `C2`, including `A1.1`, `A1.2`, `A1.3`, `A2.1`, `A2.2`, `B1.1`, `B1.2`, `B2.1`, and `B2.2`.

This differs from the CEFR descriptor workbook used earlier. The CEFR-J descriptor PDF is mainly an activity/skill can-do table. It does not provide separate descriptor scales for PROGOS categories such as Accuracy, Range, Fluency, Coherence, or Phonology. Therefore, a CEFR-J match can be strong for activity-style statements such as Overall Assessment, Interaction, and some presentation comments, while no same-category CEFR-J descriptor may exist for linguistic or phonological report categories.

For this first CEFR-J comparison, `A2 High` was checked mainly against upper-A2 CEFR-J descriptors such as `A2.2`; `B1 High` was checked mainly against upper-B1 descriptors such as `B1.2`, while also noting B1.1 overlap where the wording is interactional. The PROGOS label `B2 and above` remains less precise because the CEFR-J descriptor PDF separates `B2.1`, `B2.2`, `C1`, and `C2`, while the PROGOS report label does not identify which of those levels is intended.

Judgments below therefore use three questions:

- Does the PROGOS wording align with the reported/proxy level in the CEFR workbook?
- Does the PROGOS wording align with the reported/proxy level in CEFR-J spoken interaction or spoken production?
- If one descriptor set lacks the relevant analytic category, does another descriptor set provide a more appropriate comparison?

## Descriptor Evidence Notes

- 2026-07-06 Overall Assessment: strongest CEFR-J support is `cefrj/descriptors/CEFR-J1.0Eng.pdf`, page 2, `Spoken production`, `B1.2`, which describes a reasonably smooth presentation about personally interesting social situations and handling follow-up questions. The second sentence also overlaps with page 2, `Spoken interaction`, `B1.1`, which describes maintaining social conversation on concrete personal-interest topics.
- 2026-07-06 Interaction: strongest CEFR-J support is `cefrj/descriptors/CEFR-J1.0Eng.pdf`, page 2, `Spoken interaction`, `B1.1`, which covers opinion expression and information exchange about familiar topics using a wide range of simple English.
- 2026-07-01 Overall Assessment: CEFR-J support is mixed. The everyday/predictable-interaction wording overlaps with page 2, `Spoken interaction`, `A2.2`, while the simple-words-and-phrases wording also overlaps with lower spoken-production descriptors such as `A1.2`. This makes the match partial rather than clean.
- 2026-07-01 Interaction: `Spoken interaction`, `A2.2` and `B1.1` both overlap, but neither includes the report's condition that the other person leads the conversation. This is why the match is only partial to close.
- Accuracy and Phonology: no CEFR-J same-category match was recorded because the CEFR-J descriptor PDF checked here does not provide grammatical-accuracy or phonology scales.
- Coherence: no same-category CEFR-J match was recorded because the CEFR-J descriptor PDF checked here does not provide a coherence/cohesion scale. Any overlap comes indirectly from spoken-production descriptors about longer contributions, presentations, or structured arguments.

## Main Interpretive Consequence

The CEFR-J check adds a parallel descriptor-alignment view in two ways.

First, some PROGOS comments that looked broad or insufficiently anchored against the CEFR competence workbook have clearer activity-level parallels in CEFR-J. The clearest example is Aozawa's 2026-07-06 Overall Assessment: the statement about a reasonably smooth presentation on social issues and continuing a social conversation maps strongly to CEFR-J B1.2 spoken production plus B1.1 spoken interaction.

Second, CEFR-J does not solve the alignment problem for the six analytic categories. The CEFR-J descriptor PDF is not organised around the PROGOS analytic categories. It does not provide separate scales for grammatical accuracy, phonology, coherence/cohesion, or general linguistic range. Therefore, CEFR-J can explain some activity-style wording but cannot by itself validate the category labels or same-category analytic interpretations in the PROGOS report.

## Retained Judgment

Future report language should say that PROGOS report comments were checked against both:

- CEFR competence/activity descriptors from `cefr/CEFR Descriptors_en.xlsx`
- CEFR-J spoken interaction and spoken production descriptors from `cefrj/descriptors/CEFR-J1.0Eng.pdf`

Where CEFR-J provides a close activity-level match, report it. Where CEFR-J lacks the relevant analytic category, do not force a match. This is especially important for Accuracy and Phonology.
